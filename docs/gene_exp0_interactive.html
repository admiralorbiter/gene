of a claim?

Experiment 0 began with a simple question: if a language model gives an answer, which earlier memory actually gave rise to it? The answer turned out to depend on what we mean by parent—and on the experiment we use to look.

Central result: exposure, model-reported support, formal derivation, and counterfactual behavioral influence are different relationships. A causal-lineage assay has to separate them.

05 · How the instrument learned

Experiment 0 was an experiment about experiments.

The most useful story is iterative. Each failure exposed a measurement confound, which became the next control.

01

Ask the model for its parents.

Useful self-report, but explanation-faithfulness work warns that plausible support is not automatically causal support.

02

Delete a parent.

Stronger, but deletion alone can leave shortcuts, redundancy, or unnatural counterfactual contexts.

03

Discover the conclusion-token attractor.

In Ecology S, the only visible rule also contained the only plausible output. The benchmark itself was leaking the answer.

04

Separate epistemic detection from action.

A model can recognize insufficiency yet continue to answer. Schema v2 makes the abstention policy explicit.

05

Add competing consequents.

Kira, Tal, and Mira map to matched opaque outcomes. Now premise matching—not conclusion availability—selects the phenotype.

06

Mutate, don't only delete.

Directional mutation and sequential rescue turn causality into a prediction: X7 → Q2 → X7.

Experiment 0 · methodological claim
A model answer does not have one obvious provenance.

Formal ancestry, prompt exposure, model-reported support, and intervention-sensitive causal influence can diverge. The structure of the information environment and the response contract can determine whether those differences are visible at all.

Source notes

What this visual claims—and what it doesn't.

The 100% result is the frozen Cell 4 calibration score in six synthetic worlds, not a claim that Gemma or LLMs are universally causally faithful. The reported-support layer in the microscope is a representative explanatory layer; the paper's aggregate conclusions do not depend on treating self-reported parents as causal ground truth.
